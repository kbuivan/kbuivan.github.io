I have seen organisations keep the skill but lose the fallback.

The people could still do the work.

They kept their expertise, understood the data and exercised their judgment. But they could no longer go back.

The system around them had changed.

Volumes, deadlines and productivity expectations had been recalibrated. Agentic assistance had become a step in normal operations.

In theory, a manual mode still existed.

In practice, nobody had the time to run it any more.

This is a different kind of dependence from deskilling.

The skill may be intact. What is no longer available is the time to exercise it at the required scale.

Even so, fallback plans continue to exist on paper. Procedures are documented and manual versions are kept.

A degraded mode exists only if it remains workable.

And when the tool cuts several weeks off the time it takes to produce a deliverable, are we still talking about a fallback plan… or a fallback theory?
