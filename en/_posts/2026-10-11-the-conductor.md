Does a conductor need to be a virtuoso on every instrument to lead the musicians? I don't think so.

What a conductor needs is musicality, a sense of rhythm, and just the right firmness, so that the orchestra follows and each musician's virtuosity can be brought to light.

So when I read that leaders in the age of AI must reinvent themselves and become AI experts in their own right, I can't help questioning whether these calls are well founded.

Do we have to become a different kind of leader to stay relevant?

It's a legitimate question. But it is also, I believe, a false lead.<br>
Because when you look closely, AI doesn't change leadership.<br>
It changes the context in which leadership is exercised.

The role itself remains surprisingly stable.

What I expect from a leader is to give vision, direction and meaning when everything speeds up. To make the calls with courage and determination. To protect and develop their teams. To embody values and hold themselves to a high moral standard.

None of this is new, or technological. It is deeply human.

AI doesn't create a new kind of leadership, but it forces us to ask what makes humans irreplaceable in every trade-off and every decision.

To answer that question, the level of technological literacy required is rising.

And for me, it starts with knowing how to read the new scores that AI is writing for us.
