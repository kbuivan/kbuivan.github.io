---
title: "The pen and the intention"
ref: de-plume
line: digital-exhaustion
---

With AI, an idea for a post takes shape faster, because we assume it will take less time to write. We hesitate less before getting started. That is how I began.

I tried two methods: writing a first draft and then asking for improvements, or starting from a draft generated by the tool.

Each time, something disappeared: my tone, my rhythm, my style, sometimes even the idea I had set out to explore.

The text was nice and clean, flat, and structured to a fault. But it was no longer quite mine: the nuances my writing so often rests on had been smoothed away.

The tool kept distracting me with page after page of prose. And sometimes, in a moment of grace, a beautiful phrase would appear between two exchanges. Except that when I read it back, I found it had already shifted the meaning of what I was trying to say.

Did I save time? No. Does the tool write badly? Quite the opposite.

It's just that it eases me into a different text from the one I had in mind.

So I spend part of my time finding my own voice again. The hardest part is not forgetting what you came to say.
